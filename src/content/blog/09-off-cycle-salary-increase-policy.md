An employee receives a promotion in October, a specialist role becomes difficult to recruit for, or HR discovers a salary difference that needs attention. The organization’s annual merit cycle is not scheduled for several months. Should the company wait?

Off-cycle salary adjustments provide a way to respond outside the normal review calendar. They can be appropriate, but without clear rules they can also create inconsistent treatment and budget surprises.

### Quick answer: What is an off-cycle salary increase?

An off-cycle salary increase is a pay adjustment made outside the organization’s normal compensation review or merit cycle. It may be used for a promotion, a documented market or equity issue, a material change in responsibilities or another policy-approved reason. It should have defined eligibility, evidence, approval and budget controls.

### When should a company consider an off-cycle adjustment?

**1. A meaningful promotion or role change.** An employee takes on materially greater responsibility or moves to a higher job level, and the organization’s policy permits an adjustment before the next annual cycle.

**2. A market competitiveness issue.** Reliable market evidence indicates that the salary range or an individual’s pay is materially misaligned for a role that the organization needs to recruit or retain.

**3. An internal equity concern.** A structured review identifies a pay difference that cannot be adequately explained by consistent, job-related factors.

**4. A critical skills or retention situation.** The organization has a documented business case for retaining an employee with scarce capabilities. A competing offer should trigger a review, not an automatic counteroffer.

**5. A contractual or legal requirement.** A change may be required by employment terms, minimum-pay rules or other applicable law. HR should confirm the requirements for the employee’s jurisdiction.

**6. A correction to an earlier pay decision.** An administrative error or missed adjustment may need correction under the organization’s policy and local law.

### When should a company avoid off-cycle raises?

Avoid using exceptions as a way to bypass the normal performance process, reward the most persistent negotiator or respond inconsistently to individual manager pressure. Do not approve a permanent salary increase when a temporary allowance or acting-duty payment is more appropriate. Do not use an off-cycle adjustment to conceal a broader salary-structure problem.

### A practical off-cycle approval process

**Step 1: Require a written request.** The manager should state the reason, proposed amount, effective date, employee’s role and relevant evidence.

**Step 2: Identify the adjustment type.** Decide whether the request is a promotion, market adjustment, equity correction, retention action, contractual change or another category. This determines which policy applies.

**Step 3: Check the salary range and internal comparisons.** Review the employee’s current range, proposed salary, comparable roles and any impact on pay relationships within the team.

**Step 4: Calculate the full cost.** Include the annualized increase, prorated cost for the current fiscal year and any relevant employer-side payroll costs. Check whether the expense comes from a separate adjustment budget or the merit pool.

**Step 5: Obtain independent approval.** The manager should not be the only approver. Depending on the amount and reason, require HR, Finance and the relevant business leader to review the request.

**Step 6: Communicate and record the decision.** Confirm the approved salary, effective date and rationale. Update payroll and employee records, and retain the request and approval evidence.

### A sample off-cycle decision checklist

| Review question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is the reason allowed by policy? | Prevents exceptions becoming informal entitlements |
| Is the role and salary data accurate? | Avoids decisions based on outdated records |
| Is the proposed pay within the appropriate range? | Highlights cases requiring additional review |
| Are comparable employees treated consistently? | Reduces unexplained internal differences |
| Is the full cost funded? | Prevents unplanned payroll commitments |
| Are approvals and reasons documented? | Supports governance and future review |

### How do off-cycle increases affect the annual merit cycle?

Track them separately. If an employee receives an off-cycle market adjustment shortly before the annual merit review, decide whether the employee remains eligible for the normal cycle and how the earlier adjustment will be considered. Publish that rule in advance. Otherwise, two managers may treat similar cases differently.

An off-cycle adjustment should also feed back into future compensation analysis. Repeated requests for the same role may indicate that the salary band needs to be reviewed rather than that each employee needs a one-off exception.

### Frequently asked questions

**Are off-cycle salary increases common?**  
Practices differ by employer and sector. Some organizations tightly restrict them to a few defined reasons; others allow broader reviews with approval controls. The organization should set rules that fit its operating model.

**Can an employee request an off-cycle raise?**  
An employee may raise a compensation concern, but the employer should assess it using the same documented policy and evidence requirements that apply to other requests.

**Should a retention offer always match a competing offer?**  
No. The organization should consider role value, performance, market evidence, internal equity, replacement cost and the long-term implications of the decision.

**Who approves off-cycle salary increases?**  
A sound process usually separates the requester from the approver and involves HR and Finance. The approval levels should be defined by adjustment type and financial impact.

### Keep exceptions inside a controlled process

MeritCyc focuses on planned increment cycles, but the same governance principles apply to exceptions: clear reasons, visible cost, consistent approval and a record of what changed. Keeping off-cycle decisions visible helps the next compensation review start with accurate information.
